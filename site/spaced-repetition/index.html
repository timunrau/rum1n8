How spaced repetition helps you memorize Scripture

Spaced repetition is the practice of recalling verses from memory at spaced intervals to help them stick and increase the amount of time before you forget them.

The forgetting curve

After you learn a verse, your ability to recall it tends to decline quickly at first, and then more gradually over time. This pattern is called the forgetting curve.

Recalling things from memory resets the curve

When you successfully recall a verse from memory, it resets the curve, making it easier to remember again. It also helps flatten the curve, so that you are able to remember it longer before forgetting.

Successive recalls help flatten the curve

Each time you successfully recall a verse, it helps reset and flatten the curve a bit more. Over time, you can extend the time between recalls, eventually going for really long periods between them. But here’s the catch: it helps to wait long enough that recalling the verse takes some effort, while you can still bring it to mind. This is why it helps to progressively increase the time between recalls.

The hopeful part

Research has shown that the effort of bringing a verse back to mind can strengthen your memory of it. So when you have to stop and think before the words come back, that effort is helping the verse stick. Don’t be discouraged if it’s hard to memorize! Just keep practicing your verses each day. Over time, your "memorization muscles" will become stronger and it will be easier to memorize and recall verses.
